Behind the curtain: what drove each grade

The simulation uses a UTAUT-style model. Each grade turns its construct scores into an intention to use, with its own hidden weights. Students saw the construct scores in the pulse survey but not the weights.

What the weights say

  • Juniors respond most to social influence. They adopt because peers and managers expect it, which is also why they verify least.
  • Partners respond to performance expectancy and facilitating conditions, and barely at all to social influence. Evidence on their own clients' work (use-case libraries) and one-to-one help (reverse mentoring) move them; champions and appraisal criteria do not.
  • A mandate amplifies social influence and puts a floor under usage. But usage that comes from compliance rather than intention carries 40% less verification, and reviewers under volume pressure catch fewer errors.

What TAM and UTAUT leave out

  • Calibrated trust. Quality depends on verification habits, which run opposite to adoption across grades (Exhibit 2).
  • Use is not the outcome. The Q3 model upgrade raised trust for everyone; without calibration training, verification fell.
  • Time saved is not value. Without a capacity plan, most saved time never becomes chargeable work.
