Teaching points the simulation is built to surface

  1. The Copilot paradox. A mandate (with appraisal criteria) lifts weekly usage above 70% fast, but compliance-driven use comes with less verification. Errors reaching clients rise and staff and partner sentiment fall.
  2. Segment-specific levers. Juniors are moved by social influence, partners by performance expectancy and facilitating conditions. Champions barely move partners; use-case libraries and reverse mentoring do.
  3. Calibration as a missing construct. The Q3 model upgrade raises trust everywhere. Teams without calibration training see verification habits fall, even though the tool got better.
  4. Friction has a shadow. Verification rules and licence cuts without calibration push frustrated juniors towards public AI tools, with a risk of a confidentiality breach.
  5. Time saved is not value. Only part of self-reported time saving is real, and most of that turns into chargeable capacity only with a capacity plan.
  6. Sequencing. The strongest benchmark puts guardrails in place first, then funds enablement. It meets the insurer's deadline, wins back Harrow Lane and still reaches about 75% usage.

How the model works

1. Constructs and intention (UTAUT core)

Each grade g has scores for performance expectancy (PE), effort expectancy (EE), social influence (SI) and facilitating conditions (FC) on a 1–7 scale, starting from Exhibit 2. Its weighted score is:

score_g = w_PE·PE + w_EE·EE + w_SI·SI·m + w_FC·FC        (m = 1.3 under a mandate)

Target usage moves from the observed baseline through a logistic link, and actual usage closes 60% of the gap each quarter:

target_g = σ( logit(baseline_g) + 1.2 · (score_g − score_g,0) )
intent_g ← intent_g + 0.6 · (target_g − intent_g)

Usage is then adjusted for policy: a mandate sets a floor (grade-specific), the Restructuring ban suppresses 6.7% of each grade by 85%, and right-sizing caps licences at 1,400 users.

2. Levers

Each lever adds max × multiplier_g × (1 − e^(−spend/scale)) to a construct every quarter, so returns diminish within a quarter. Gains in SI and FC decay by 8% a quarter towards their starting point unless they are sustained. Policies modify constructs only while switched on.

3. Quality (automation-bias layer)

error rate (AI)   = 0.28 × retrieval(0.7) × model upgrade(0.75) × citation ban(0.8)
                    × (1 − 0.5·verify_drafters) / (1 − 0.5·verify_drafters,0)
catch rate        = 0.39 + protocol(0.15) + 0.10·calibration level
                    + 0.2·(manager verify − 0.52) − mandate(0.05)
escaped errors    = 3,200 × AI share × error rate × (1 − catch) + non-AI escapes
client incidents  ~ Poisson(escaped × 0.003)

Verification by staff who use the tool only because of a mandate is 40% lower. AI share of deliverables is driven by usage among grades 1–2, seniors and managers.

4. Value

real hours saved  = Σ users_g × self-reported minutes_g × realisation      (realisation 0.55 → 0.85 max)
chargeable hours  = real hours × conversion                                  (0.25, or 0.35 with a capacity plan)
utilisation       = 68.4% + Δ chargeable hours / 990,000 hours

Customising the simulation

All parameters live in two files in the repository: assets/js/data.js (grades, levers, policies, events, benchmark strategies, score weights) and assets/js/model.js (model constants at the top of the file). After editing, run node tests/model.test.js to check that the baseline still reproduces the case and every benchmark fits the budget.
