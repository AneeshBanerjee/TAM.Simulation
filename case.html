Case briefing

A summary of the full teaching case. Meridian Hale LLP and all named individuals are fictional; data are constructed for teaching.

The situation, November 2026

Meridian Hale LLP is a UK top-15 accounting and advisory partnership with £412m in fee income and 2,400 people across 11 UK offices and Dublin. On 2 March 2026 it gave every employee Meridian Assist, an enterprise generative AI assistant connected to the firm's knowledge base. Use was voluntary. Year-one spend was £2.1m, justified by a promise to release 8% of chargeable capacity within 18 months.

By September, weekly usage had stalled at 57% against a 70% target. Juniors used it daily, while only 19% of partners used it in a given week. Restructuring banned it for client work in July after a misstated statutory deadline. In September, Harrow Lane Capital found two fabricated "comparable transactions" in a due diligence report. Meridian Hale cut £85,000 from the fee and Harrow Lane paused new instructions.

Users reported saving 34 minutes a day, yet chargeable utilisation rose only from 68.0% to 68.4%. A quality review found that AI-assisted deliverables were better written but more than three times as likely to contain an unverified or incorrect factual claim, and reviewers caught fewer of those errors.

Who thinks what

Daniel Osei, COO: "We've paid for 2,400 licences and 1,000 of them are shelfware. Put it in the workflow templates and the appraisal competencies, and usage will follow."
Dr Priya Nair, Head of Quality & Risk: "Mandating usage before we've defined what good usage looks like would turn an adoption problem into a quality problem at scale."
Raj Mehta, CIO: "We trained people on how to prompt, not when to rely on it. We never taught that boundary."
Graham Pell, Audit Partner: "Nobody has shown me a use case for my day. And if partners start using it heavily, what do we tell clients we're charging for?"
Aisha Rahman, Associate: "My managers expect the speed now. Do I check every source? I check the ones that look wrong."
Malcolm Reid, Head of Restructuring: "Give me a tool that tells me when it's unsure, and an audit trail, and I'll reconsider."
