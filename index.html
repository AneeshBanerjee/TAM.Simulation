Meridian Hale: The Copilot Paradox

Eight months after giving all 2,400 staff an AI assistant, a UK accounting and advisory firm is stuck at 57% weekly usage. Partners barely use the tool, one practice has banned it, and a client has found fabricated sources in a report. You have one year and £2.4m to make adoption work. The question is what "work" should mean.

Your role

You are the AI Programme Board advising Catherine Okafor, Managing Partner. The partnership board has approved a year-two budget of up to £2.4m, including licences. Each quarter of 2027 you will allocate investment, switch firm policies on or off, and respond to events. At the end of the year the board will judge you on a balanced scorecard, not just the usage figure on the vendor dashboard.

1. Read the briefing

2. Play four quarters

Each round takes 10–15 minutes. You will see a quarterly pulse survey, usage by grade, quality results and events. Your decisions carry forward.

3. Debrief

The debrief shows your scorecard, reveals what really drove each grade's behaviour, and compares your strategy with five benchmark strategies.

What you can do each quarter

Invest

  • AI Champions network
  • Practice use-case libraries
  • Partner reverse mentoring
  • Prompting and skills training
  • "When to rely" calibration course
  • A verified-retrieval feature (one-off build)

Set policy

  • Mandate AI steps in workflows
  • Add AI use to appraisals
  • Verification protocol and "AI-assisted" flag
  • Ban AI-generated citations in client work
  • Lift the Restructuring ban
  • Right-size licences to 1,400
  • Capacity reinvestment plan

The scorecard

The board will weigh six outcomes: adoption (weekly active users), appropriate reliance (use combined with verification), quality (factual errors reaching clients), value (time saved that becomes chargeable capacity), risk (client incidents, breaches, insurance) and people (staff sentiment and partner support).
